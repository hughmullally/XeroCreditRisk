# The Cost of Late Payment to UK SMEs — Research Notes

*Compiled to answer "is there a concrete number for the cost of credit risk to
UK SMEs?" — a gap flagged in [email-reply-cofounder.md](email-reply-cofounder.md).
Five independent sources, cross-checked rather than taken at face value.*

## Headline Answer

- **Most rigorous estimate**: late payment costs the UK economy **almost £11
  billion per year**, causes **14,000 business closures per year** (38/day),
  and **1.5 million businesses (28%)** are affected annually — from a
  government-commissioned, academically-reviewed study.
- **Broader "everything currently overdue" estimate**: **£100bn+** is
  outstanding in late payments across the UK SME economy at any given time,
  consistently reported by three separate accounting platforms analysing
  their own real transaction data (Xero £141bn, Sage £112bn).
- These aren't contradictory — they answer different questions (see
  [Reconciling the Numbers](#reconciling-the-numbers) below).

## Source 1: London Economics / GOV.UK (primary, most rigorous)

**"Late Payments Research: Impact on the UK Economy"** (July 2025), commissioned
by the Department for Business and Trade & the Office of the Small Business
Commissioner, conducted by London Economics with academic advisors from
Warwick Business School and Aston Business School. Based on YouGov/IFF
Research survey fieldwork plus econometric analysis (Propensity Score
Matching against the Longitudinal Small Business Survey).

### Headline findings

- Costs the UK economy **almost £11 billion per year** (~0.4% of GDP)
- **Over 1.5 million businesses (28%)** affected by late payments each year
- Businesses collectively **owed an estimated £26 billion** at any given time
  — average **£17,000 per affected business**
- **22%** of businesses spend staff time chasing late payments — averaging
  **86 hours per affected business per year** (133 million hours economy-wide)
- **14,000 businesses close every year** because of late payments — **38 per
  day** — accounting for an estimated **40,000 jobs**
- **15%** of businesses have stopped doing business with specific customers
  because of their payment behaviour

### Cost breakdown (of the ~£11bn total, central estimates)

| Cost component | Central estimate (£m/year) |
|---|---|
| Increase in business closures | 3,214 |
| Decrease in investment | 1,714 |
| Staff time spent chasing unpaid debtors | 2,259 |
| Debt collection | 1,132 |
| Legal cost | 1,213 |
| Supply chain finance / invoice discounting / factoring / selling overdue invoices | 1,213 |
| **Total** | **~10,745** |

### Investment foregone, by business size (as % of annual turnover)

| Size | Investment foregone (businesses affected) | As % of turnover |
|---|---|---|
| Micro (0–9 employees) | £6,103 | **3.06%** |
| Small (10–49) | £19,170 | 0.54% |
| Medium (50–249) | £126,187 | 0.51% |
| Large (250+) | £77,015 | 0.03% |

**Micro businesses lose the largest share of their turnover to foregone
investment** — over 6x the rate of small businesses and 100x the rate of
large ones. This is the clearest evidence in the whole report that late
payment hits the smallest businesses hardest, proportionally.

### Sector breakdown

Construction has the highest rate of late payment being a **"big problem"**
(10.1%), followed by Business Services (9.1%). The FSB's own August 2025
data independently corroborates this — construction, professional services,
and retail were named as the hardest-hit sectors.

### One counterintuitive nuance

**Larger businesses report more late-payment issues than micro businesses**
(59% of large businesses face both overdue invoices and 60+ day terms, vs.
12% of micro businesses) — but the report attributes this to micro
businesses being less likely to extend trade credit in the first place, not
to the problem being less severe for them. The investment-foregone data
above shows the opposite once a micro business *is* affected.

### Other findings worth knowing

- A **10% reduction** in poor payment practices could be worth **almost £1
  billion/year** in economic benefit
- **84%** of late payments (by volume) faced by UK businesses are from **UK
  customers**, not international ones

## Source 2: FSB (Federation of Small Businesses)

Survey of 2,000+ small businesses, October–December 2024, plus an August
2025 update.

- **45%** experiencing more late payments than 12 months ago
- **24%** receive payments up to 60 days late
- **52%** simply write off/forfeit late payments up to 10 times a year
  rather than chase them
- **60%** say late payments are holding back business growth
- **63%** spend time chasing overdue payments — costing up to **£5,200/year**
  in lost time and resources
- August 2025 update: **54%** paid late in the past three months, worsening
  for 1 in 5 — **construction, professional services, and retail** hit
  hardest (corroborates the London Economics sector data)

## Source 3: Xero Small Business Insights

Xero's own transactional data across its UK customer base.

- Average UK small business owed **£24,841** in late payments at any time
  (~10% of average turnover)
- Extrapolated across the UK SME economy: **£141 billion** outstanding
- Cost of late payments to UK SMEs: **£1.6 billion in 2023** (narrower
  financing/interest-cost calculation, not the same scope as London
  Economics' £11bn)
- Estimated **50,000 small businesses fail each year** due to cash-flow
  issues caused by late payments

## Source 4: Sage (via CEBR)

Commissioned analysis by the **Centre for Economics and Business Research**
of **1.2 million+ real anonymised invoices from 31,000+ Sage customers** —
actual transactional data, not a survey.

- UK's smallest businesses owed an average of **£42,000** in overdue
  invoices — risen for **three consecutive years**
- **44%** of invoices paid late
- **£112 billion** currently locked up in late payments across the UK SME
  economy
- **49%** of SME invoices overdue, average **27 days** after issuing before
  payment arrives
- SMEs themselves take longer to pay *their own* suppliers too — averaging
  **37.1 days**, up from 31.9 days a year earlier (evidence of the problem
  cascading down supply chains)

## Source 5: QuickBooks (Intuit)

"Small Business Insights" — international quarterly survey (UK, US, Canada)
since September 2021.

- 2025 UK report: businesses with unpaid invoices owed an average of
  **£21,000+** each
- **54%** have at least 1% of invoices overdue by 30+ days; on average
  **11%** of invoices are 30+ days overdue
- Earlier report: average owed **£27,214**, 17% of invoices overdue
- **70%+** of SMBs impacted by extended payment terms or late payments in
  the past year
- **~40%** fear potential business closure if late payments persist

## Reconciling the Numbers

The five sources don't agree on headline totals, and that's worth being
upfront about rather than cherry-picking the most flattering figure:

| Metric | London Economics (Gov) | Xero | Sage | QuickBooks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total owed/outstanding at any time | £26bn | £141bn | £112bn | not aggregated to a UK total |
| Average owed per affected business | £17,000 | £24,841 | £42,000 | £21,000–27,214 |
| Annual "cost" figure | ~£11bn | £1.6bn | — | — |
| Businesses failing per year | 14,000 | 50,000 | — | — |

**Why they diverge**: the three accounting platforms (Xero, Sage,
QuickBooks) are counting **every invoice currently overdue by any amount**,
drawn from real transaction data across large customer bases — a broad,
point-in-time snapshot. London Economics is deliberately narrower — it
isolates businesses where late payment is specifically a **"big problem"**
causing measurable economic harm (closures, foregone investment), which is
a much smaller, more conservative subset by design.

**Recommended framing for the pitch**: *"Estimates range from £26bn (severe,
economically-damaging cases only — government-commissioned) to £100bn+ (all
overdue invoices at any moment — per Xero's and Sage's own transaction
data). Even the conservative end represents a huge addressable problem."*
Lead with the London Economics £11bn/14,000-closures figures as the most
defensible headline number — it's the only one that's independently
peer-reviewed and methodologically transparent — and cite the platform
figures as supporting evidence of scale, not as equally precise numbers.
